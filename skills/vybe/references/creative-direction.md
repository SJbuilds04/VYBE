# Creative direction

The difference between a generated slideshow and a piece of motion design is
made here, before any HTML exists. Work as a director pitching to a client.

## Method

1. **Find the single idea.** Write the concept as one sentence that a viewer
   could repeat ("Light finding its shape — every scene is darkness yielding to
   form"). If you cannot, you do not have a concept yet.
2. **Pick a visual metaphor** that carries the idea across scenes (a line that
   becomes a graph that becomes a logo; an orb that the camera circles; a word
   that becomes the frame of the next scene). Continuity sells polish.
3. **Generate three directions, choose one.** Briefly consider a safe, a bold
   and an unexpected take. Pick the strongest that still fits the audience. Do
   not average them.
4. **Define the system** — palette, type, motion language, camera, transitions,
   texture, sound — so every later decision is a lookup, not a guess.

## Anti-generic checklist

- No stock structure "title → three bullets → logo" unless the idea demands it.
- No centered text over a flat gradient as the main visual. Build a world.
- No identical entrance animation for every element.
- No more than one fade-through-black per piece (act breaks only).
- Copy is specific to the subject. Placeholder phrases ("Revolutionize your
  workflow") are a failure.
- At least one moment the viewer will remember (a reveal, a transformation, a
  camera move through the subject).

## `creative-direction.md` template

```markdown
# Creative direction — <title>

**Concept.** <one sentence>
**Metaphor / through-line.** <what carries across scenes>
**Audience & feeling.** <who, and what they should feel at the end>
**Directions considered.** Safe: … · Bold: … · Unexpected: … → chosen: … because …

## Visual identity
- Style: <preset | blend | custom> — <why>
- Palette: bg #… · surface #… · ink #… · accent #… · accent-2 #… (contrast AA checked)
- Typography: display <font, weight, tracking> · body <font> · mono <font>
- Texture & lighting: <grain? glow? studio light? flat graphic?>
- Depth stack: background … · midground … · foreground …

## Motion language
- Easing: entrances <ease>, moves <ease>, exits <ease>
- Rhythm: beat ≈ <s>; cut on <music beats | narration phrases>
- Signature move: <the one motion idea the piece is known for>
- Camera: <dolly, orbit, truck, crane, zoom-through, parallax…> per act
- Transitions: <list, varied>

## Sound direction
- Music: <mood, tempo, instrumentation, arc (build/drop/resolve)>
- SFX: <families and where they hit>
- Voice: <none | provider + voice + pacing, and why>

## Risks
- <what could look cheap and how we avoid it>
```

Keep it under ~60 lines. It is a decision record, not an essay.
